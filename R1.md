# Architectural Synthesis for Post-Backpropagation Artificial General Intelligence
## A Hybrid Neuro-Symbolic Framework

### 1. Introduction: The Imperative for Hybrid Architectures
The trajectory of Artificial Intelligence research has historically oscillated between two distinct poles: the symbolic paradigm, characterized by explicit rule-based reasoning and high interpretability, and the connectionist paradigm, currently dominated by deep neural networks utilizing backpropagation. While the latter has achieved unprecedented success in perceptual tasks and pattern recognition, it faces increasing scrutiny regarding its biological plausibility, energy efficiency, and capacity for reasoning under uncertainty without catastrophic forgetting. The "Black Box" nature of end-to-end differentiable models complicates belief management, logical consistency, and safety verification, while the reliance on global error gradients (backpropagation) imposes severe constraints on real-time, local learning in distributed hardware.

This report presents an exhaustive analysis of the theoretical and technical foundations required to construct a hybrid cognitive architecture that eschews end-to-end backpropagation in favor of biologically plausible, local learning rules. By synthesizing literature across classical cognitive architectures (ACT-R, Soar, LIDA), emerging graph-based frameworks (OpenCog Hyperon, Sigma), and neuro-symbolic mechanisms (Vector Symbolic Architectures, Predictive Coding), we outline a blueprint for a system capable of temporal cognition, belief revision, and embodied decision-making. The proposed architecture integrates fast associative activation (System 1) with deliberative symbolic reasoning (System 2), unified through a distributed hypergraph substrate and regulated by economic attention mechanisms.

The analysis is structured to systematically deconstruct the components of cognition—memory, learning, control, and reasoning—and reconstruct them using mechanisms that support "learning without backpropagation." This involves a critical re-examination of Hebbian plasticity, temporal difference learning, and truth maintenance systems, positioning them not as historical artifacts but as essential components of a robust, energy-efficient AGI.

### 2. The Landscape of Cognitive Architectures
To design a novel hybrid architecture, one must first deconstruct the mechanisms of established systems to identify reusable components and failure modes. The field has matured through the "Common Model of Cognition" (CMC), yet distinct approaches to memory, control cycles, and learning remain, offering critical insights for a post-backpropagation framework.

#### 2.1 Classical Symbolic and Hybrid Architectures
The foundational architectures—ACT-R, Soar, and CLARION—represent the standard for modeling human-like intelligence. They differ fundamentally in how they bridge the gap between implicit processing and explicit reasoning, providing a rich catalog of mechanisms for System 1 and System 2 integration.

##### 2.1.1 ACT-R (Adaptive Control of Thought-Rational)
ACT-R posits a unified theory of cognition based on the interaction between a central procedural memory (production rules) and distributed declarative memory modules (buffers). It is arguably the most biologically grounded of the classical symbolic architectures, mapping distinct functional modules to specific brain regions.

*   **Mechanism and Interaction:** The architecture utilizes a modular design where distinct buffers—visual, motor, goal, and retrieval—communicate via a central production system. This structure explicitly models the separation of concerns in the cortex, with the production system functioning analogously to the basal ganglia, gating information flow between cortical regions. System 1 processes occur intra-modularly (e.g., the rapid retrieval of a memory chunk based on vector similarity), while System 2 processes emerge from the sequential firing of production rules that manipulate these chunks over time.
*   **Learning and Utility:** Crucially for a non-backpropagation framework, ACT-R employs sub-symbolic learning mechanisms that do not rely on global gradients. It uses "utility learning," a form of reinforcement learning where the utility of a production rule is updated based on the success or failure of the outcome. This mechanism allows the system to resolve conflicts between competing rules using a Bayesian-like selection process, effectively modeling habit formation and procedural optimization. Furthermore, "base-level activation" decay models biological forgetting, ensuring that frequently accessed knowledge remains accessible while irrelevant data fades, a distinct advantage over the static weights of standard neural networks.
*   **Scaling Limitations:** Despite its success in modeling human reaction times and error rates, ACT-R struggles with scaling to massive knowledge bases. The symbolic nature of its production rules can lead to a combinatorial explosion, and the system lacks a native mechanism for handling high-dimensional raw sensory data without significant preprocessing layers. Memory allocation overhead becomes a critical bottleneck as the problem space expands, often requiring "micro-theories" rather than a unified general intelligence.

##### 2.1.2 Soar
Soar emphasizes a centralized working memory and a universal decision cycle, contrasting with ACT-R’s distributed buffer system. It frames all cognition as a search through problem spaces, aiming for a "Grand Unification" of intelligent behavior.

*   **The Impasse-Driven Learning Cycle:** Soar’s most significant contribution to the proposed hybrid architecture is its mechanism for transitioning between System 1 and System 2. When immediate knowledge is sufficient to select an operator, the system acts automatically (System 1). However, when knowledge is insufficient or conflicting, an "impasse" occurs. This impasse triggers a recursive sub-state (System 2 deliberation), where the system uses deeper search or reasoning to resolve the lack of knowledge. Once resolved, the result is "chunked" into a new rule, automating the response for future encounters. This mechanism, known as Explanation-Based Learning (EBL), allows for one-shot learning of complex behaviors, a capability often absent in gradient-based deep learning.
*   **Memory Structure:** Unlike ACT-R’s explicit buffers, Soar uses a unified working memory represented as a graph. Long-term knowledge is partitioned into procedural (rules), semantic, and episodic memories. This graph-based working memory aligns well with modern neuro-symbolic approaches, although Soar’s reliance on explicit symbol manipulation makes grounding in noisy, continuous environments challenging without "transducer" layers to discretize sensory inputs.

##### 2.1.3 CLARION
CLARION (Connectionist Learning with Adaptive Rule Induction ON-line) explicitly addresses the neuro-symbolic divide by distinguishing between implicit and explicit processes in a dual-layer architecture.

*   **Dual-Process Integration:** CLARION consists of a top level (explicit/symbolic) and a bottom level (implicit/connectionist). Uniquely, it facilitates bidirectional learning:
    *   **Bottom-Up Learning:** Implicit knowledge accumulated in the connectionist layer (via reinforcement learning) is "extracted" into explicit rules in the top layer.
    *   **Top-Down Assimilation:** Explicit rules provided to the system guide and constrain the learning of the neural networks below.
    This architecture demonstrates that backpropagation is not strictly necessary for hybrid learning; local association and rule extraction can drive the system. It provides a robust model for how a "System 1" associative network can train a "System 2" symbolic reasoner, and vice-versa, without requiring a unified differentiable loss function.

##### 2.1.4 LIDA (Learning Intelligent Distribution Agent)
LIDA provides the most biologically faithful model of the cognitive cycle, grounded in Global Workspace Theory (GWT). It offers a temporal template for the proposed architecture, defining how asynchronous processing leads to synchronous consciousness.

*   **The Cognitive Cycle:** LIDA operates in discrete, cascading cycles, typically running at 5–10 Hz. Each cycle consists of a sequence: Perception $\rightarrow$ Workspace (Preconscious) $\rightarrow$ Competition $\rightarrow$ Conscious Broadcast $\rightarrow$ Action Selection. This cycle offers a solution to the "binding problem" in distributed systems by defining a discrete moment of integration.
*   **Consciousness as Functional Broadcast:** The "conscious" step in LIDA is a global broadcast of the most salient coalition of information to all modules. This broadcast serves two critical functions: it recruits resources to handle the current situation, and it gates learning. In the proposed non-backprop architecture, this broadcast serves as the "global modulator" signal for Hebbian learning (discussed in Section 4), ensuring that only salient, attended events drive synaptic plasticity.

#### 2.2 Emerging Graph-Based and Probabilistic Architectures
While classical architectures rely on production rules, modern approaches are moving toward flexible, graph-centric, and probabilistic models that are better suited for distributed computing and uncertainty management.

##### 2.2.1 Sigma ($\Sigma$)
Sigma attempts a "Grand Unification" by rebuilding cognitive architecture on factor graphs and piecewise linear functions. It generalizes symbols and probabilities into a uniform graphical model.

*   **Mechanism:** In Sigma, "thinking" is the process of solving the graph—computing marginals via the Summary-Product Algorithm (a generalization of the Belief Propagation used in Bayesian networks). This allows Sigma to perform logical inference, probabilistic reasoning, and constraint satisfaction using the same computational substrate. For a hybrid system, Sigma demonstrates that graph traversal and message passing can replace distinct "modules," offering a mathematically rigorous way to handle uncertainty.

##### 2.2.2 OpenCog Hyperon and the AtomSpace
OpenCog represents the most ambitious attempt at a fully graph-based AGI. Its core component, the AtomSpace, is a weighted, typed hypergraph where nodes (Atoms) represent concepts, percepts, or logic.

*   **Distributed AtomSpace (DAS):** Recent developments in OpenCog Hyperon have introduced the Distributed AtomSpace, which allows this massive hypergraph to be sharded across distributed infrastructure. This addresses the scaling limits of single-machine memory that plagued earlier architectures like ACT-R. Algorithms such as Pattern Mining and Logic Inference operate locally on shards and return results to a working memory "cache".
*   **Cognitive Synergy:** The key insight from OpenCog is "Cognitive Synergy": the idea that multiple algorithms (Probabilistic Logic Networks for reasoning, MOSES for evolution, ECAN for attention) can operate on the same hypergraph simultaneously. This prevents the "module isolation" problem and allows for a dynamic interplay between reasoning and learning.

##### 2.2.3 NARS (Non-Axiomatic Reasoning System)
NARS is built on the premise of insufficient knowledge and resources. Unlike architectures that assume a "God's eye view" of truth, NARS utilizes a Non-Axiomatic Logic where truth is evidence-based (defined by frequency and confidence).

*   **Control and Belief:** NARS employs a priority-based bag system for tasks and beliefs, ensuring that the system remains responsive even when overwhelmed. This form of resource-bounded rationality is essential for embodied agents operating in real-time. Its ability to revise beliefs based on new evidence makes it inherently robust to uncertainty and contradiction, a critical requirement for System 2 belief management.

### 3. Representational Substrates: Graphs and Vectors
The choice of data structure dictates the capabilities of the mind. To avoid end-to-end backpropagation while maintaining the flexibility of neural networks and the precision of symbolic logic, the proposed architecture utilizes a dual-coding scheme: a distributed Hypergraph augmented with Vector Symbolic Architectures (HDC/VSA).

#### 3.1 The Graph-Theoretic View: AtomSpace and Hypergraphs
The core memory of the system is a labeled, weighted hypergraph. Unlike standard knowledge graphs which are limited to triples (Subject-Predicate-Object), hypergraphs allow edges (Links) to connect an arbitrary number of nodes.

*   **Hyperedges for Higher-Order Logic:** Hyperedges are essential for representing n-ary predicates and higher-order relationships found in natural language and complex reasoning. For example, the relationship "Alice gave Bob a book in the library" involves four entities and cannot be naturally represented by binary edges without reification. In a hypergraph, a single Give link can connect {Alice, Bob, Book, Library}. This structural capability supports the rich semantic representations required for System 2 deliberation.
*   **Type System and Ontology:** Every node and link in the graph is strongly typed (e.g., ConceptNode, EvaluationLink, HebbianLink). This allows the system to distinguish between "ontological reality" (Concept A exists) and "epistemic probability" (A is likely true). The AtomSpace model enables the graph to serve as a unified repository for both declarative knowledge (facts) and procedural knowledge (executable schema), facilitating the seamless interaction between learning and reasoning.

#### 3.2 Vector Symbolic Architectures (HDC/VSA)
While graphs excel at structure, they often struggle with "fuzziness," similarity, and noise tolerance. Hyperdimensional Computing (HDC), also known as Vector Symbolic Architectures (VSA), provides the mathematical bridge between the neural (vector) and symbolic (graph) worlds.

##### 3.2.1 The Algebra of Thought
VSA operates on high-dimensional vectors (hypervectors), typically with dimensionality $D \ge 10,000$. These vectors are holographic, meaning information is distributed across all dimensions, making them inherently robust to noise and hardware faults.

*   **Core Operations:** The power of VSA lies in its algebraic operations that manipulate symbols without losing their structured relationships:
    *   **Bundling (Superposition):** Element-wise addition ($C = A + B$). This represents sets or collections. The resulting vector $C$ is similar (in terms of cosine distance) to both $A$ and $B$.
    *   **Binding:** Element-wise multiplication or XOR ($Z = A \otimes B$). This represents associations or variable binding (e.g., Role-Filler). Crucially, the result $Z$ is orthogonal (dissimilar) to both $A$ and $B$, effectively creating a new symbol.
    *   **Permutation:** Shifting the vector elements ($P = \Pi(A)$). This encodes sequences or directed edges, enabling the representation of order and hierarchy.

##### 3.2.2 Solving the Symbol Grounding Problem
A critical failure mode of pure symbolic AI is the "Symbol Grounding Problem": how do abstract symbols acquire meaning from the physical world? VSA provides a mathematical solution via continuous mapping.

*   **Mechanism:** Raw sensory data (e.g., pixel outputs from a neuromorphic camera) can be mapped into the high-dimensional space. The "symbol" for Dog is not an arbitrary identifier but the superposition (bundling) of all Dog visual vectors ever encountered by the agent ($V_{Dog} = V_{sight1} + V_{sight2} + \dots$). Because bundling preserves similarity, the resulting symbol $V_{Dog}$ retains the topological structure of the sensory manifold. It is "grounded" in perception. This allows the system to perform symbolic logic on vectors that contain rich, sub-symbolic sensory information.

#### 3.3 Neuro-Symbolic Integration: The Vector-Atom
The proposed architecture synthesizes these approaches by annotating every Graph Node (Atom) with a dynamic VSA Vector. This Vector-Atom dual representation enables two distinct modes of cognition:
1.  **Explicit Reasoning (Graph Layer):** Algorithms traverse the explicit edges of the hypergraph to perform logical deduction (e.g., A $\rightarrow$ B $\rightarrow$ C). This corresponds to System 2 deliberative thought.
2.  **Implicit Association (Vector Layer):** Activation spreads through the graph based on the vector similarity of nodes, even if no explicit edge exists. If $V_{NodeA}$ is similar to $V_{NodeB}$ (high dot product), activating A will "ignite" B. This corresponds to System 1 fast associative memory and intuition.

*   **Content-Addressable Memory:** This structure functions as a content-addressable memory. To retrieve information, the system does not need a memory address; it queries the graph with a "probe vector." The system returns all nodes whose vectors resonate with the probe, enabling rapid, heuristic recall of relevant context.

### 4. Learning Mechanisms Without Backpropagation
Abandoning backpropagation necessitates alternative mechanisms for credit assignment and weight adjustment. The literature supports a multi-scale approach: Local Hebbian Rules for fast association and Predictive Coding for hierarchical error correction.

#### 4.1 Three-Factor Hebbian Learning
Hebbian learning—"cells that fire together, wire together"—is the biological standard for synaptic plasticity. However, pure Hebbian learning is unsupervised and insufficient for goal-directed behavior. The Three-Factor Rule introduces the necessary control signal to guide learning toward useful outcomes.

##### 4.1.1 The Mechanism
The weight update $\Delta w_{ij}$ for a synapse connecting neuron $j$ to neuron $i$ is defined as:
$$\Delta w_{ij}(t) = \eta \cdot f(\text{pre}_j(t), \text{post}_i(t)) \cdot M(t)$$

*   **Factors 1 & 2 (Correlation):** The function $f$ detects coincidence between pre-synaptic and post-synaptic activity. In Spike-Timing Dependent Plasticity (STDP), this function is asymmetric: if the pre-synaptic neuron fires before the post-synaptic neuron, the weight is strengthened (Long-Term Potentiation - LTP). If it fires after, the weight is weakened (Long-Term Depression - LTD). This causality-encoding mechanism is crucial for learning temporal sequences.
*   **Factor 3 (Modulation $M(t)$):** This is the critical innovation for reinforcement learning. $M(t)$ represents a global or regional signal such as Reward, Punishment, Surprise, or Novelty. In the brain, neuromodulators like Dopamine and Acetylcholine serve this function. This factor "gates" plasticity, ensuring that the system only learns associations that are relevant to its goals.

##### 4.1.2 Eligibility Traces and Temporal Credit
A major challenge in reinforcement learning is the Temporal Credit Assignment Problem: the reward often arrives seconds or minutes after the crucial action was taken. Hebbian coincidence, however, occurs on the millisecond timescale.

*   **Solution:** Synapses in the architecture maintain a decaying chemical "eligibility trace" ($e_{ij}$) of their recent activity. The weight update is then deferred until the modulator arrives:
    $$\Delta w_{ij}(t) = \alpha \cdot M(t) \cdot e_{ij}(t)$$
    When the reward signal $M(t)$ is broadcast, it "stamps in" all synapses that have active eligibility traces, bridging the temporal gap. This mechanism mathematically approximates TD($\lambda$) in Reinforcement Learning, allowing the system to learn complex temporal sequences without Backpropagation-Through-Time (BPTT).

#### 4.2 Predictive Coding: The Error-Driven Engine
Predictive Coding (PC) offers a powerful, biologically plausible alternative to backpropagation for learning hierarchical representations.

*   **Mechanism:** In PC, the brain is viewed as a prediction machine. Each layer in the hierarchy generates a prediction of the activity in the layer below it. The difference between the top-down prediction and the actual bottom-up input constitutes the Prediction Error.
*   **Free Energy Minimization:** The system minimizes "Free Energy" (surprisal) by updating weights locally to reduce this prediction error.
*   **Local Gradients:** Crucially, the gradients required for these updates are local. A neuron only needs to know the activity of its immediate neighbors (prediction from above, error from below) to calculate the weight change. This avoids the "weight transport problem" of backpropagation, where neurons must know the weights of downstream layers.
*   **Graph-Based Predictive Coding:** Recent research has extended PC to arbitrary graph topologies. In the proposed architecture, nodes in the hypergraph attempt to predict the state of their neighbors. This allows the network to perform inference (filling in missing information) and learning (updating edge weights) simultaneously. PC supports bi-directional information flow—top-down expectations meet bottom-up sensation—enabling the system to handle cyclic graphs and recurrent connections, which are difficult for standard feedforward backprop networks.

#### 4.3 Modern Hopfield Networks (Dense Associative Memories)
Modern Hopfield Networks (MHNs) serve as the system's Associative Memory engine, providing a robust mechanism for pattern retrieval and noise reduction.

*   **Capacity and Retrieval:** Unlike classical Hopfield networks, which have limited storage capacity ($\approx 0.14N$), MHNs utilize continuous states and steep activation functions (e.g., softmax) to achieve exponential storage capacity ($C \approx \exp(N)$). This allows them to store massive numbers of patterns (VSA vectors).
*   **Function:** MHNs act as "clean-up" memories. When a noisy or partial vector is retrieved from the graph (e.g., a corrupted visual input), it is fed into the MHN. The network dynamics naturally settle into the closest stored attractor (canonical state), effectively "denoising" the symbol.
*   **Learning:** The energy landscape of the MHN is shaped by Hebbian-like updates (imprinting patterns into the weight matrix), making them fully compatible with the non-backprop constraint.

### 5. Belief Management and Truth Maintenance
System 2 cognition requires more than just association; it requires the ability to maintain explicit beliefs, reason about their validity, and handle contradictions without system failure. Unlike neural networks, which opaque "black boxes," this architecture utilizes Distributed Truth Maintenance and Non-Axiomatic Logic.

#### 5.1 Distributed Truth Maintenance System (DTMS)
Traditional Truth Maintenance Systems (like de Kleer's ATMS) are computationally expensive and centralized. A Distributed TMS fits the sharded graph architecture of the AtomSpace.

*   **Mechanism:** Each node in the graph maintains a "Label" consisting of its Justifications—the IDs of the premise nodes that support it. If a premise is revoked (e.g., a sensor reveals that "The door is open" is false), a revocation message propagates through the graph links recorded in the Justification set, invalidating dependent nodes. This ensures that the system's beliefs remain consistent with the current evidence.
*   **Handling Contradiction:** When two nodes representing contradictory states (e.g., $A$ and $\neg A$) are simultaneously active with high importance, a Conflict is detected. This triggers a System 2 "Impasse" (similar to Soar).
*   **Resolution:** The system resolves the conflict using Energy Minimization (analogous to the Hopfield Net dynamics). The conflicting subgraph is treated as a constraint satisfaction problem. The network relaxes into the state that violates the fewest constraints or has the highest aggregate support (Short Term Importance). This effectively solves the "Cognitive Dissonance" problem via physics-inspired dynamics.

#### 5.2 Non-Axiomatic Logic (NAL)
To handle the uncertainty of the real world, the architecture adopts the logic of NARS.

*   **Evidence-Based Truth:** In NAL, truth is not a binary value (True/False) but a tuple $\langle f, c \rangle$, where $f$ is frequency (proportion of positive evidence) and $c$ is confidence (amount of total evidence).
*   **Revision Rule:** When the system encounters new evidence for a belief, it uses the NAL revision rule to merge the new tuple with the existing one. This allows the system to incrementally become more confident as it gathers data, or to reduce confidence if it encounters contradictory evidence.
*   **Robustness:** This approach makes the system inherently robust to contradiction. Instead of crashing when $A$ and $\neg A$ are asserted, the system simply updates the truth values to reflect the mixed evidence, allowing it to function in "insufficient knowledge and resource" conditions.

### 6. Temporal Cognition and Decision Making
Time is not merely an index in a sequence; it is a fundamental dimension of cognition. The architecture must explicitly represent and reason about time to perform planning and causal inference.

#### 6.1 Phase Precession and Sequence Compression
A critical question in cognitive modeling is how learning rules that operate on milliseconds (STDP) can learn behavioral sequences that span seconds. The answer lies in Theta Phase Precession.

*   **Mechanism:** As an agent moves through a sequence of states (e.g., passing landmarks $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C$), neurons in the hippocampus fire in a specific relationship to the theta brain rhythm (5–10 Hz). As the agent enters field $A$, cell $A$ fires late in the theta cycle. As it moves toward $B$, cell $A$ fires earlier and earlier.
*   **Compression:** This phenomenon compresses the temporal sequence of events ($A \dots B \dots C$) into a single theta cycle ($A-B-C$ firing in rapid succession).
*   **Learning:** This compression brings the events $A$ and $B$ within the roughly 20-50ms window of STDP. This allows the synapse $W_{AB}$ to strengthen, effectively encoding the prediction "A leads to B." This mechanism allows the architecture to learn the topological structure of time and causality purely through local plasticity.

#### 6.2 Time Cells and Episodic Memory
*   **Time Cells:** The architecture incorporates specialized nodes known as "Time Cells," which fire sequentially during delay periods. These cells bridge the temporal gap between a cause (Conditioned Stimulus) and a delayed effect (Unconditioned Stimulus), enabling the system to learn associations across time gaps where no external sensory change occurs.
*   **Episodic Replay:** Episodic memories are traces of the "Conscious Broadcast." They are stored as linked chains of VSA vectors in the graph.
*   **Offline Consolidation:** During low-load periods (simulated sleep), these chains are reactivated in reverse or forward order ("Replay"). This replay reactivates the Hebbian links, consolidating the episodic traces into stable semantic schemas. Crucially, replay allows the Reinforcement Learning system to propagate credit back from rewards to early actions (TD-Lambda) without requiring the agent to physically repeat the actions.

#### 6.3 Decision Making: Temporal Difference (TD) Learning
Decision making is driven by the interaction of Model-Free (habitual) and Model-Based (goal-directed) systems.

*   **Basal Ganglia and TD Learning:** The Basal Ganglia module is responsible for action selection. It estimates the value $V(s)$ of the current state.
*   **Dopamine as Error Signal:** The system calculates the TD-Error $\delta(t) = r(t) + \gamma V(t+1) - V(t)$. This scalar value $\delta(t)$ is globally broadcast to the graph.
*   **Action Selection:** It serves as the modulator $M(t)$ in the 3-factor Hebbian rule. Synapses connecting State nodes to Action nodes that were active (via eligibility traces) are strengthened if $\delta > 0$ and weakened if $\delta < 0$. This implements standard Reinforcement Learning without backpropagation.
*   **Model-Based Bias:** For novel situations where habits fail, the system uses its learned Graph (World Model) to simulate future states via spreading activation. The "imagined" value of these future states acts as a bias signal to the Basal Ganglia, allowing the agent to make decisions based on predicted outcomes rather than just cached values.

### 7. Attention and Control
Cognition requires dynamics—flow, control, and attention—to manage the "firehose" of sensory data.

#### 7.1 The Cognitive Cycle: LIDA-based Timing
The system operates on a fundamental frequency, implementing a LIDA-like cognitive cycle (approx. 10 Hz) to synchronize distributed graph updates.

*   **Phase 1: Perception (0-80ms):** Sensory buffers bundle raw VSA vectors. Feature detectors (PC networks) settle on low-level interpretations.
*   **Phase 2: Local Association (80-150ms):** Percepts move to the "Global Workspace." Spreading activation (ECAN) pulls in associated Long-Term Memory nodes.
*   **Phase 3: Competition (150-200ms):** Coalitions of nodes compete for dominance.
*   **Phase 4: Broadcast (200ms):** The winning coalition "ignites" and is broadcast to all modules. This broadcast triggers the 3-factor learning rules globally.
*   **Phase 5: Action:** The broadcast triggers matching Procedural Rules.

#### 7.2 Economic Attention Allocation (ECAN)
The AtomSpace is too large to process fully every cycle. Economic Attention Allocation (ECAN) acts as a resource scheduler, treating attention as a scarce currency.

*   **STI (Short Term Importance):** Nodes possess STI. Percepts are gifted initial STI.
*   **Dynamics:** STI spreads via Hebbian links to neighbors. Nodes pay a "tax" (decay) every cycle. Nodes with STI dropping below a threshold are effectively "forgotten" (removed from the active workspace). This prevents graph explosion and ensures the system focuses only on the most relevant sub-graphs.

#### 7.3 Thalamocortical Gating and Biased Competition
To prevent chaos, the Thalamus (simulated) gates information flow.

*   **TRN Inhibition:** The Thalamic Reticular Nucleus (TRN) is modeled as a "Winner-Take-All" (WTA) network. It inhibits competing sectors of the thalamus. If "Visual Object A" is active, the TRN suppresses "Visual Object B."
*   **Top-Down Bias:** The Prefrontal Cortex (Goal nodes) sends excitatory bias vectors to the TRN, lowering the threshold for task-relevant items. This allows the agent to "look for red things" (Top-Down Attention), biasing the competition in favor of current goals.

### 8. Embodiment and Critical Analysis
Cognition cannot be separated from the sensorimotor loop. The proposed architecture is designed for Embodied Agents, distinguishing it from disembodied Large Language Models (LLMs).

#### 8.1 Embodied Grounding and Benchmarks
*   **Proprioceptive Schema:** The graph must contain a "Body Schema" subgraph representing the agent's kinematic state (joint angles, velocity). Concepts like "left," "heavy," or "reach" are grounded not just in vision but in these motor vectors. This grounding is essential for manipulating the physical world.
*   **Benchmarks:** Evaluation must move beyond static datasets. The system should be tested in environments like Animal-AI or BEHAVIOR, which assess "animal-like" common sense—object permanence, tool use, and causal reasoning—rather than just linguistic fluency. These benchmarks test the system's ability to integrate perception, memory, and physics in real-time.

#### 8.2 Critical Failure Modes and Synthesis
*   **The Scaling Limit:** A primary failure mode of symbolic systems is the combinatorial explosion of rules.
    *   *Mitigation:* The System 1 (Spreading activation/VSA) acts as a heuristic filter. It prunes the search tree massively before the System 2 logic engine (PLN/NARS) attempts to reason. The logic engine only sees the tiny subgraph "lit up" by attention.
*   **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Deep learning models suffer from catastrophic forgetting because backpropagation changes weights globally.
    *   *Mitigation:* The proposed architecture uses Local Learning. Updating the weights of the "Coffee" concept node does not affect the "Tea" node if their VSA vectors are orthogonal. Furthermore, Generative Replay (dreaming) during idle cycles ensures old memories are refreshed and integrated with new knowledge.

| **Symbol Grounding** | Symbols become detached from reality | **VSA Integration:** Symbols are superpositions of sensory vectors |
| **Combinatorial Explosion** | Graph search becomes intractable | **ECAN Pruning:** Heuristic attention restricts search space |

#### 8.3 Conclusion
The research indicates that a robust hybrid cognitive architecture without backpropagation is not only feasible but necessary for the next leap in AGI. By integrating the Cognitive Cycle of LIDA, the Hypergraph of OpenCog, the Vector Algebra of HDC, and the Local Learning of Hebb/PC, we arrive at a design that is transparent, energy-efficient, and biologically plausible. This architecture moves beyond the "black box" of deep learning, offering a path toward general intelligence that is grounded in the physical world and capable of rigorous reasoning.
