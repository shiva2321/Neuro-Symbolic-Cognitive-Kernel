Discuss what these sources say about Node-level, in the larger context of Graph Tokenization.

In the context of Graph Tokenization, the node-level approach is identified as the most granular strategy, treating each individual vertex in a graph as a discrete token. This method is particularly effective for tasks where the focus is on node-specific attributes, such as node classification, rather than the overarching topology of the network.
The sources provide the following insights into node-level tokenization and its role within broader graph architectures:
1. Granularity and Taxonomy
Node-level tokenization is situated at the finest end of a taxonomy that also includes edge-level, hop-level, and subgraph-level tokenization. While it allows models to capture high-detail information about individual units, it often struggles to express global graph structural patterns or long-range dependencies unless supplemented with additional positional embeddings.
2. The Scalability Bottleneck
A primary limitation cited for node-level tokenization is its quadratic computational complexity (O(N 
2
 )), which arises when applying self-attention over all pairs of node tokens. This constraint effectively limits the use of traditional Graph Transformers to relatively small structures, such as molecular graphs, as they struggle to scale to real-world networks with millions of nodes.
3. Challenges in Semantic Granularity
The sources distinguish between the "low-level" semantics of individual nodes and the "high-level" semantic tokens found in other modalities like language (words) or vision (patches). Because single nodes often lack a natural sequence structure, they are difficult for pure Transformers to process effectively without a mechanism to convert them into high-level semantic units.
4. Innovations in Node Representation
To overcome these limitations, several frameworks redefine the node's role in tokenization:
• Node Energy and Selection: In systems like MuseGraph, a "node energy" metric (H(v)) is used to prioritize the most semantically rich and structurally central nodes for inclusion in a "compact graph description" to fit within Large Language Model (LLM) token limits.
• Graph-to-Sequence Transformation: Models such as GraphsGPT and G2PM transform non-Euclidean node structures into "Graph Words"—Euclidean sequences of learnable vectors—using random walks. This allows the model to treat node-based data as a pure language sequence.
• Multimodal Integration: In Neural Graph Memory (NGM), node-level tokenization involves fusing multiple modalities (vision, text, audio) into single episodic nodes that serve as persistent memory anchors.
5. Theoretical Expressivity
From a theoretical standpoint, node-level tokenization is compared to the Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) test hierarchy. While a pure Transformer treating nodes as tokens equals DeepSets in expressive power, enhancing node tokens with structural positional encodings (like Laplacian eigenvectors) allows them to achieve higher-order expressivity and distinguish non-isomorphic graph structures.
